searchable.com: the smartest site in our study, and the one caught not doing what it sells

Searchable Limited (London, founded 2025, $14M seed from Freestyle Capital) sells AI-search visibility tracking. The product tells you whether ChatGPT and Perplexity can see and cite your brand. They went 0 → 2,229 organic visits in six months on only 156 URLs, which is the single most replicable growth story we found. They also serve GPTBot a 532-byte stub where a human gets 565KB, and their DR 48 rests on a PBN. Both halves are worth your time: the first is the playbook, the second is the warning. 1 · The number to explain: 0 → 2,229 in six months, on 156 pages

They did not out-produce anyone. 156 pages is smaller than most agency blogs. What they did was publish the dictionary for a category two to four months before the category had search volume, so when "AEO" and "AI visibility" inflected in H1 2026, the pages were already sitting at the top with no incumbent to displace. The content did not get better. The queries caught up to it.

The transferable version

Your category has the same window right now. Terms like agentic ops, tool use, agent memory, eval suite, guardrail, agent drift, confidence gate are pre-volume today. A 40–60 entry glossary sprint costs a fraction of one pillar page, ranks against nobody, and is the cheapest compounding asset available to a DR-0 domain. The lag is real. Searchable ate six months of zero, but the six months were going to pass anyway.

2 · The authority play: one real expert, hired first

A brand-new domain cannot buy expertise signals. Searchable rented them, openly and legitimately.

  • Shaun Anderson: founder of the Hobo SEO Blog (running since 2006, independently well-known), 20+ years in technical SEO, credited as Advisor, not staff. His six articles are the earliest-dated posts on the entire blog: deep technical engagement with the Google Content Warehouse leak, contentEffort, siteAuthority, predictedDefaultNsr. Not generic filler · checkable, specific, and correct.
  • A disclosed 16-person named bench: co-founders plus freelancers credited by name with real bios ("How-To Geek contributor, 100+ published articles"). Outsourced, and said so.
  • Writer + reviewer bylines: top posts carry "written by Jason Dookeran, reviewed by JP Garbaccio, Head of SEO & AEO." The pattern borrowed from medical/YMYL publishing.
Why this is the highest-leverage thing on their site, and directly yours

Compare: one competitor bylined 83% of posts to a persona with no photo. Another put one CEO on 490 articles spanning HIPAA to frontend internals. Both collapsed. Searchable found one real, Googleable practitioner, credited them prominently, and paired staff writing with their named review.

You have 50 engineers doing real delivery work. That is 50 checkable practitioners, a bigger genuine bench than Searchable bought. Author pages with Person schema, real photos and LinkedIn links, on day 2 of the build.

And the part that undercuts it

/customers carries three real, attributed case studies with named founders and hard numbers ("£1m+ pipeline, 206% share of voice"). Below them sits a logo wall · American Express, Siemens, Pfizer, KPMG, BCG, H&M, Wayfair · under "Powering 1,000+ ambitious teams", with no case study, no quote, no named contact on a single one. These are very plausibly free-report users. It is the largest trust liability on an otherwise credible site, and it is the one thing on this page you should not copy. Separately: the review badge reads 98 reviews on the homepage and 93 on a feature page, crawled the same day.

Their own post describes the logic: serve a colocated machine.md if one exists, else fall back to a generic template. The homepage · the highest-authority URL on the domain · is running on the fallback. A random blog post gets 8–18KB of rich markdown; the front door gets a stub.

4a · Three compounding failures, each independently verifiable

  • CriticalThe FAQ answers never reach any AI crawler. On both sampled posts, the markdown contains ## Frequently Asked Questions followed by a completely empty div. We cross-checked the page's own FAQPage JSON-LD and the answers exist and are good · "GEO is not the same as SEO, but there is considerable overlap. Around 80-90% of GEO and AEO practices come from traditional SEO fundamentals…" That answer exists only in the JS-hydrated version. GPTBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot never see it. The single most citable content on the page is the content that fails to arrive.
  • CriticalThe stripping pass leaks its own scaffolding. Orphaned </KeyTakeaways> closing tags with no opening tag; "Six myths and misconceptions to consider:" followed by an empty <div className="not-prose"> where the six myths should be; on the second post, at least 8 empty divs where stat cards and a "Trust Chain" diagram used to be. The loss is not random · it strips exactly the structured, schema-marked, stat-card content that is most citation-friendly, and leaves plain prose intact.
  • High/mcp · their own MCP Agent product page · has zero server-rendered content. 1,593 characters of raw HTML, 100% nav and footer. No <h1>. No product copy. And it gets the generic 532-byte markdown fallback too. Neither path carries anything. The page selling AI-agent compatibility is invisible to AI agents. Note that GPTBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot do not execute JavaScript · that is published in their own crawler documentation.
The rule this hands you, for free

Build the pattern, then test what actually arrives. A markdown mirror is the right architecture. We recommend it. But a team that sells AI visibility, that wrote the public blog post about this exact system, shipped it with empty FAQ blocks on their best content and a stub on their homepage, and did not notice.

Your Day-30 sweep runs curl -A "GPTBot" yoursite.com/<page> on ten pages and reads what comes back. Then repeats for OAI-SearchBot · the crawler behind live ChatGPT citations, missing from Searchable's list and from most robots.txt files you will ever see.

5 · DR 48: and what's holding it up

Ahrefs reports 35,377 live backlinks from 996 referring domains. 35.5 links per domain. An organic profile runs 2–3. We traced the gap.

"I remember when searchable.com was barely getting noticed online, just a few dozen visitors each month. After trying SEOExpress.org for their SEO packages, I saw my traffic leap to over 3,000 monthly visitors in just two months!…"
verbatim anchor text on 205 referring domains, flagged is_spam: true

That is the same spun testimonial found on three other sites in this study. One vendor is quietly fulfilling "SEO packages" across this entire market segment.

  • The farm, named: seogear.shop, seoprime.shop, rankcart.shop, backlinkorbit.shop, seodepot.shop, ranktrove.shop, rankstock.shop, seopicks.shop, primebacklinks.shop, seotrove.shop, linkwares.shop, rankgear.shop, primeseo.xyz and a dozen more · each DR 0.7 or below, each contributing an almost identical 87–91 links. 23 of the top 30 domains by volume (77%) are seller-branded .shop/.xyz shells.
  • They did not hide it. Live URL slugs include backlinkorbit.shop/x3v5cv1-best-pbn-backlinks-pharmaceutical-2025-compared-6-providers-roi/. The words "pbn-backlinks" are in the URL.
  • ~26 more anchors are the vendor's own sales copy, auto-varied around a fake compound token: "Complete SEO for brandsearchable.com: high-quality backlinks, guest posts… delivering a strong DR/DA/TF increase in any language." Then adminsearchable.com, salessearchable.com, funnelsearchable.com · 20+ variants, 41–44 domains each, all flagged spam.
  • The fingerprint in one line: the homepage has 945 referring domains and a URL Rating of 6.0 against a site-wide DR of 48. Genuinely earned links from 945 domains would put UR far higher. Mass low-power injection, not authority.
What's actually real underneath

A genuine layer does exist: google.com, wikipedia.org, forbes.com, prnewswire.com, g2.com, crunchbase.com, similarweb.com, builtin.com, techtimes.com, each giving 1–4 links, the exact shape of normal press, directory and job-board mentions for a funded young SaaS. Roughly 100–150 real referring domains. Our estimate: DR 26–32 is durable; 16–22 points are manufactured and likely to erode as .shop clusters get deindexed.

Their 2,229 visits are almost certainly earned by the content, not the links · which makes the purchase both risky and, apparently, unnecessary.

The one line to take from this section

The best-executed content strategy in this study still bought links. When a vendor offers you DR growth, this is what you are buying, a farm of .shop shells running spun testimonials with your domain in them, sitting in Google's link-spam crosshairs. You now know the fingerprint: check links-per-domain, and check homepage UR against site DR.

6 · Verdict: and the six things to copy

Searchable is the most sophisticated operator we audited and the clearest proof that the strategy in your roadmap works: a six-month-old domain, 156 pages, no programmatic play, beating established agencies in a category it defined. It is also proof that sophistication does not prevent shipping a broken pipeline or buying a bad link package.

  1. Publish the dictionary before the category has volume. 40–60 glossary entries, batched. Zero competition, ranks by the time demand arrives, cheapest compounding asset a DR-0 site has.
  2. One real, checkable expert as your first author. Plus a disclosed named bench and a writer+reviewer byline on every post. You have 50 engineers; this costs you nothing but a photo and a schema block.
  3. Ship the machine layer on day zero, robots.txt naming every AI crawler individually, a spec-correct llms.txt that links your best pages, and OAI-SearchBot in the list.
  4. Build the markdown mirror, then verify it. curl -A "GPTBot" and read the output. Their FAQ answers vanish; yours must not.
  5. A free instant diagnostic, result before email. Their visibility report is the loop; your cost calculator and readiness scorecard are the same mechanic.
  6. Never buy links, and never claim a logo you cannot back with a quote. The two things that cost them here are the two things that cost nothing to avoid.

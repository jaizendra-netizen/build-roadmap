1 · Automation company vs AI agent development company: the real difference

This is not a naming exercise. They are different products, different buyers, different price points, and different competitive fields.

Automation company

  • What it sells: deterministic workflows, "if X then Y." Zapier/Make/n8n builds, RPA, integrations, data pipelines.
  • Who defines the steps: you do. Every branch is drawn in advance by a human.
  • Fails when: reality doesn't match the flowchart: a new invoice format, an unusual request, an exception nobody mapped.
  • Buyer: ops manager / IT lead. Budget: $5–50k projects.
  • Competition: enormous and commoditized: every freelancer on Upwork, every no-code shop. Reflected in the keywords: "ai automation agency" KD 43, "ai workflow automation" KD 63.

AI agent development company

  • What it sells: goal-driven systems: an LLM with tools, memory and guardrails that decides the steps.
  • Who defines the steps: the agent does, within limits you engineer.
  • Handles: the exceptions: an invoice in a format never seen, a request phrased three ways, a judgment call escalated to a human.
  • Buyer: CTO / VP Ops / Head of Digital Transformation. Budget: $50k–500k+ builds, plus retainers for evals, monitoring, iteration.
  • Competition: young and fragmented: "ai agent development company" KD 17, and the SERP still has room (proof in §2).
The one example that makes it concrete

Automation: "When an invoice email arrives → extract the PDF → run OCR → if amount < $1,000 auto-approve → else route to a manager." Beautiful until an invoice arrives as a photo in the email body. Then it breaks and a human fixes it.

Agent: "Handle incoming invoices." It reads the email, recognises an invoice in a format it has never seen, extracts the fields, notices the amount is unusual for that vendor, checks the contract, and asks a human with its reasoning attached. Same business problem, but one is a flowchart and one is a colleague.

Commercially that difference is everything: the flowchart is a quote-and-deliver project competing on price. The colleague is an engineering engagement with evals, monitoring and iteration: recurring revenue, higher ticket, fewer credible competitors.

So which do you call yourself? Both: in a specific order

With 50 people you can deliver both, and you should. But the label is a strategic choice, and the data is unambiguous:

  • Position as an AI agent development company. Young category, KD 17, $10 CPC, buyers with CTO-level budgets, and no incumbent has owned it yet. Appinventiv only started their agent cluster in mid-2025.
  • Sell automation as the entry product. Bigger volume of buyers, shorter sales cycle, faster cash, and it's the natural first project that becomes the agent engagement six months later. It lives on your site as a service page under your brand (/ai-automation-services/), not as your identity.
  • Never lead with "we automate everything." That phrase competes with Upwork freelancers, ranks for KD 43–63 terms, and gives a CTO no reason to pay you enterprise rates.

2 · How keyword difficulty actually works: and where it lies

Plain mechanics first. Ahrefs KD is a 0–100 estimate of one thing only: roughly how many referring domains the pages currently in the top 10 have. KD 5 ≈ the top pages have a handful of links. KD 50 ≈ the top pages have ~100+ referring domains each. That's it. It is a backlink estimate, nothing more.

Which means it ignores: domain authority, brand recognition, content depth, topical authority, freshness, and whether Google simply trusts IBM more than it trusts you. And that is why KD misleads in both directions. Here is the live proof from your own market, pulled today:

Correction to my own earlier advice

In the previous research I called "enterprise ai agents" (2,250 searches, KD 5) your biggest easy win. I checked the actual SERP today and I was wrong. Page one is IBM (DR 92), Google Cloud (DR 99), Gumloop (DR 74), Sema4 (DR 61), Sanalabs (DR 67). The KD reads 5 only because those giants rank with very few links to those specific pages. Gumloop's page has 2 backlinks. They don't need links; they have brand. A DR 0 site does not win that SERP in year one, whatever the KD says. This is exactly the "SERP-verify before committing" caveat, and I should have run the check before recommending it. Treat it as a year-two target.

The rule that replaces KD · use this forever

Don't ask "what's the KD?" Ask "is there anyone at my level on page one?" Open the SERP, check the Domain Ratings of the ten results. If two or more sites sit within ~20 DR points of where you'll be in six months, the keyword is winnable, build the page. If page one is uniformly DR 70+ brands, walk away no matter how low the KD reads. KD is a useful first filter for building a list; the SERP check is the decision. And what "DR 0" means in practice: you have no accumulated trust yet, so you win only where Google is still willing to rank unknown sites, which, in the AI-agent category right now, is most of the buyer-intent long tail. That window closes as the category matures, which is the real argument for moving fast.

6 · Direct answers to your three questions

  1. "Can we increase posts per month?" Yes 20–30/month is right for your headcount, but only under one condition: every piece carries a real practitioner's byline and at least one thing that could only come from your delivery work (a real number, a real failure, a real architecture). The moment it becomes 30 generic AI-written posts you have rebuilt APPWRK's September 2024: 110 posts, then a 95% collapse. Volume with proof compounds; volume without proof is a time bomb.
  2. "Can we increase the GEO/AEO strategy?" Yes, substantially, and it's your fastest channel because AI engines don't care about domain age. The scaled program: the technical baseline (llms.txt, /ai-info, valid schema, server-rendered) + monthly prompt tracking (log which brands ChatGPT/Claude/Perplexity name for your 30 money questions. That's your real rank tracker) + quarterly original data + one interactive tool per service line + directory/review saturation. Remember the TechGropse lead that came from ChatGPT: it happened because they were embedded in directories, not because they optimized anything.
  3. "Best product-market fit to overtake Appinventiv?" Not by out-publishing them on mobile-app-development topics. They own that with a decade of links. You overtake by owning the category they entered only 14 months ago, from a position they structurally can't hold: they are an app-development company that added AI agents; you are an AI agent company with 50 engineers and no legacy positioning to protect. Their AI-agent cluster is 41 posts and 2 service pages. Build 300 pages of genuine agent depth in 18 months and you don't overtake their traffic: you take their category, which is where the market is moving anyway.

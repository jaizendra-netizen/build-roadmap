WordPress vs Webflow for your AI‑automation site: what the data actually says

We inspected the live code, sitemaps, and Ahrefs traffic of workflows.io, TechGropse, and 14 comparable companies. Three of the four assumptions behind this decision turned out to be wrong — and the platform question turns out to be the smallest question in the room.

Verdict

Choose WordPress (self-hosted, Yoast or RankMath, Polylang/WPML for ES·PL·DE) — but understand that the data shows platform explains almost none of the outcome. The #1 site in the scan runs Webflow (Copy.ai, 139k visits/mo) and the #2 runs WordPress (Appinventiv, 107k visits/mo); both platforms also host sites with 1,600+ posts and under 1,000 visits. WordPress wins for your specific constraints: four target countries (unlimited locales at fixed plugin cost vs per-locale Webflow fees), unlimited pages for programmatic SEO (Webflow caps CMS items), the proven stack of the winners in your exact agency niche, and a small non-engineering team.

The bigger finding: the strategy you planned to copy is dead. TechGropse hit 65,845 visits/mo in Nov 2023 — exactly your 50k–100k target — and Google's 2024 updates erased 99% of it. Copying their publishing model on any platform reproduces their collapse, not their peak.

1 · What we verified about your two reference sites

workflows.io is not built on Webflow. The homepage source shows a custom Next.js application deployed on Vercel (_next/static chunks, Vercel deployment IDs). More important: Ahrefs shows workflows.io had zero organic search traffic until December 2025 and sits at ~163 visits/mo today, ranking for 5 keywords. Their client acquisition engine is the founder's LinkedIn brand, not their website. They are a marketing benchmark, not a technology benchmark — nothing about their stack choice tells you anything about SEO.

TechGropse is only half WordPress. The main site — 481 pages of services and ~60 country/city landing pages — is custom-coded (Bootstrap, no CMS). Only the blog at /blog/ runs WordPress 6.9 with Yoast. So "TechGropse ranks because of WordPress" was never true; their SEO ran on hand-built country pages plus a WordPress content farm bolted on the side.

Same country, same niche, same algorithm — quality of target and maintenance discipline is the entire difference.

The pattern that actually predicts traffic — every site above 20k visits/mo has all three; every site below 1k is missing at least two:

  • Domain Rating 64+ — real backlinks. Every winner is DR 71–86; every loser is DR 38–58.
  • Buyer-intent topics — the losers rank for junk (GB WhatsApp, buffstreams); the winners rank for "AI agent for X", "how much does Y cost".

4 · The multi-country dimension (US · ES · PL · DE)

This is where your requirements actually separate the platforms — and where the market is empty: not one of the 16 sites does real hreflang localization. The only locale folders found anywhere were 7 URLs on Bacancy. Everyone competes in English and lets Google sort out the geography. Spanish, Polish and German content for AI-automation services is an open lane — a genuine 2–3-month opportunity, because you'd be competing against translated nothing.

5 · What to actually build (the part that determines the outcome)

Recommended stack: WordPress on managed hosting (Kinsta / WP Engine / Cloudways), lightweight theme (GeneratePress/Blocksy — not Elementor page-soup like SoluLab), RankMath or Yoast, WPML or Polylang with proper hreflang, WP Rocket. Total ~$40–70/mo. Everything in this stack is operable by a non-engineer.

Copy the winners' architecture, not TechGropse's blog farm:

  1. Programmatic service × use-case × industry pages — "AI automation for [insurance claims / invoice processing / lead routing]", "n8n / Make / Zapier automation agency", integration-pair pages like Lindy's. These are your Copy.ai/Lindy equivalent, and they are buyer-intent by construction.
  2. Geo pages for buying markets — a handful of deep country pages (US, DE, ES, PL, UAE) like Appinventiv/Code Brew, localized properly, not 60 thin city clones.
  3. 5–8 genuinely deep posts/month — LeewayHertz publishes least in the winner group and converts best. Cost guides, "how we built X" teardowns, comparison pages. Never chase volume keywords outside your buyer's world.
  4. Refresh, don't just publish — Appinventiv updates ~550 old posts a month; that maintenance discipline is what recovery from algorithm updates looks like.
  5. Backlinks from day one — DR 64 is the observed entry ticket. Founder content, tool directories, integration-partner listings, guest data studies. Without this, no platform ranks you.
  6. ES/PL/DE localization of your best 20 pages in month 2–3 — the uncontested lane nobody in the cohort occupies.

Reality check on "inbound in 2–3 months": on a fresh domain, months 2–3 can deliver long-tail bottom-funnel rankings (cost/comparison/integration queries) and localized-market rankings — not head terms. TechGropse needed ~14 months to reach 17k/mo even in 2023's much easier algorithm. Plan for LinkedIn + outbound to carry pipeline while SEO compounds; that is exactly the workflows.io lesson.

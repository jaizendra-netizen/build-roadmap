2 · How the pages link to each other (the part that decides if it works)

Genesys had 365 posts and 94% of them linked to nothing · that's why they died. Appinventiv's blogs each push 2–3 links into service pages · that's why they win. Here is your linking law, drawn:

Reads top to bottom. Red dashed = manufactured or low-quality. Green = genuine and earning. Grey dashed = dead weight.

The five linking rules. Put these in your content brief so nobody forgets

1. Every blog post: minimum 2 links to a service/industry page, 1 to a case study, 2–3 to sibling posts. No exceptions. A post that can't link to a money page shouldn't be written.
2. Every service page: links to 3–5 case studies (as Challenge/Solution/Impact strips), its sub-services, the cost guide, and the industries it serves.
3. Every case study: links back to the service it proves + its industry page + 2 sibling cases. Never a dead end.
4. One conversion destination: /book-a-call/. Every CTA on the site points there. No competing forms, no scattered Calendly embeds.
5. Never orphan: if a page exists, at least 3 other pages link to it. Run a monthly check · if a URL has zero internal links, either link it or delete it.

5 · The non-negotiables (taped above the developer's desk)

  1. One domain. Countries are subfolders (/ae/, /de/). Never a second domain · that was TechGropse's escape route from a burned site, not a strategy.
  2. No year in any URL. /guide/ai-agent-development-cost/, never …-2026/. Put the year in the title tag and refresh annually · the URL keeps its authority forever.
  3. Every page has one job and one keyword family. Two pages must never target the same query (APPWRK ran five cities with two competing pages each).
  4. Real bylines, real bios, real photos. No "Ray Hudson" ghost authors, no single CEO byline on 490 articles.
  5. Schema validated once per template. Run every template through the Rich Results Test before it ships. All three broken sites shipped their bug in a template.
  6. Nothing covers the fold. No timer popups. Trust badges live on the page, not inside a modal.
  7. Never buy links. The stats asset and directory profiles are the plan. If a vendor offers DR growth, they're reselling the network we caught three times.
  8. Refresh calendar from day one. Every published page gets a review date 4–6 months out, in the same sheet as the publishing calendar.

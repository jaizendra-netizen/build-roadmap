onegtmlab.com: 871 pages, 365 referring domains, zero organic traffic

This is the audit we ran on every competitor, turned on ourselves. The result is blunt: the site ranks for nothing. Not a few keywords. Zero. And the cause is not effort or volume, it is three specific defects, two of which are a single afternoon's work to fix. There is also a finding about our backlink profile that needs a decision this week.

1 · Why nothing ranks: the 797 tool pages

The tool directory is 91.5% of the site. We pulled fourteen at random and crawled them directly. Every one came back the same way.

What a page needs to rankA unique <title> naming the thing · an <h1> · a <link rel="canonical"> · enough distinct content that Google can tell it apart from its siblings · ideally schema describing what it is
What all 14 sampled tool pages haveTitle: OneGTM, identical on every one · H1: none, on all 14 · Canonical: none · JSON-LD: none · Body: 97.3% identical to each other, 95% of the characters shared

Google is being handed 797 pages whose title tag is the same three-word string, that carry no heading, and whose visible text is 95% boilerplate. There is no signal available to distinguish /tools/lemlist-c6c2e from /tools/scalelist. This is not a ranking problem. It is a page-identity problem. The pages cannot rank because there is nothing on them that says what they are.

The precedent, from our own audit set

APPWRK built 36 city pages that measured 98–99% identical. Their traffic fell 95%, from 25,700 a month to 1,185, and eighteen months of good content afterwards did not recover it. Google's scaled-content-abuse policy does not penalise volume. It penalises pages that are undifferentiated. Ours are at 97.3%.

We have not been penalised. We were never ranked in the first place, so there was nothing to lose. That is the one piece of luck here: the fix is additive, not a recovery.

1a · The three-line fix

ChangeWhereEffort
Bind the title to the tool name: {Tool} pricing, features and alternatives | OneGTMLabWebflow CMS collection page settings~15 min
Add an H1 bound to the tool name fieldSame template~10 min
Add a self-referencing canonicalSite-wide, not just tools~10 min
Add 120–200 words of genuinely per-tool copy: what it costs, who it is for, one real limitation, what we would pair it withNew CMS field, 797 rowsthe real work
Add SoftwareApplication schema per toolTemplate embed~30 min

The first three take under an hour together and are worth doing today regardless. The fourth is the one that decides whether the directory earns anything: 797 × 150 words is roughly 120,000 words, which is a real content programme, not a template change. Our own roadmap already sets this rule for the integration pages: no engineer-supplied specifics for a tool means do not build that page. The directory was built without applying it.

2 · The backlink profile is the vendor we have been documenting

365 referring domains produce DR 2.3. That gap is the same fingerprint we flagged on four competitors. We pulled the anchor text.

"Before I found SEOExpress.org, I had nearly given up on im…"
· the anchor on 217 of our 365 referring domains

A further 40 domains carry "onegtmlab.com gets high-quality backlinks, guest posts, on-page…", which is the vendor's own sales copy used as anchor text. The referring domains themselves are: buybacklinks.agency, backlinker.shop, seoexpress.org, rank-optimizer.website, linkrankpro.shop, rankxlinks.shop, seorankflow.shop, buyseobacklinks.shop, authoritybacklinks.shop, rank-top.click. This is the fifth site in our study carrying the SEOExpress network, after Genesys, Omnibound, APPWRK and Searchable. It is the first one that is ours.

Two readings, and the honest position

Either someone bought a package at some point, or the vendor is using our domain as filler in its own spam content. The second is genuinely plausible and probably more likely: the anchor text is a testimonial for SEOExpress, not a promotion of us. That is what a link farm writes when it needs an outbound link to look natural. We are not in a position to say which from the data, and this audit does not.

The action is the same either way. Disavow the cluster in Search Console. It is passing nothing (DR 2.3 on 365 domains is proof of that), it costs nothing to remove, and leaving a documented link farm pointing at us while we publish audits naming that exact network on other people's sites is not a position worth holding.

4 · The rest of the gap list

  • CriticalNo canonical tag anywhere on the site, not just on tools. Checked on the homepage, a service page and a case study. With 797 near-identical pages this is the difference between Google picking one and Google trusting none.
  • CriticalNo meta description and no og:title on the homepage. Google writes its own snippet, and every LinkedIn or Slack share renders without a title card. For a company whose primary channel is LinkedIn, that is a daily cost.
  • HighNo llms.txt, no /ai-info. Both 404. We recommend these in the roadmap's machine layer and have not shipped them ourselves.
  • HighOnly 7 blog posts and 1 services page in a sitemap of 871. The commercial pages that would actually convert are outnumbered 100:1 by directory entries that convert nothing.
  • MediumNo security headers: no CSP, X-Frame-Options, X-Content-Type-Options, Referrer-Policy or Permissions-Policy. Not a ranking factor; it is a due-diligence question an enterprise buyer's security team will ask.
  • MediumSitemap served as application/rss+xml rather than application/xml.
  • NoteWebflow. Consistent with the platform recommendation in the roadmap for a site this size; nothing here argues for a migration.

5 · What to do, in order

  1. This week, under two hours: per-tool titles, an H1 on the tool template, self-referencing canonicals site-wide, a homepage meta description and og:title. These are template edits in Webflow and they unblock every other thing on this list.
  2. This week, one hour: disavow the SEOExpress cluster in Search Console. Export the referring domains from Ahrefs, keep the genuine ones (substack.com and the real directory listings), disavow the .shop / .website / .click farm.
  3. Decide what the tool directory is for. 797 pages with 150 unique words each is a 120,000-word programme. If that is not going to happen, cut it to the 100 tools we actually have an opinion on and noindex the rest. A hundred pages that rank beat 797 that do not.
  4. Then the commercial layer. One services page against 797 tool pages is the wrong ratio. The roadmap's own linking law says ~14 money-page links to 3 blog links; our own site is nowhere near it.
  5. Ship the machine layer: llms.txt pointing at the services, case studies and tool directory, plus /ai-info. We advise this; we should run it.
The honest summary

We built 871 pages and earned zero visits, because 797 of them were shipped without a title, a heading or a reason to exist separately. Nothing here is a strategy failure, and nothing needs rebuilding. The first hour of work is template fields. The real decision is whether the tool directory gets the 120,000 words it needs or gets cut to the hundred entries we can actually write.

Worth noting: this is the exact mistake the roadmap already warns about, in the programmatic-pages section, sourced from the APPWRK collapse. We wrote the rule and did not apply it to ourselves. That is the most useful thing this audit found.

Method: Ahrefs API v3 (domain-rating, metrics, metrics-history, backlinks-stats, refdomains, anchors, organic-keywords), full sitemap parse of 871 URLs, direct crawl of 22 pages with browser and AI-crawler user agents, pairwise body-similarity measurement across 14 randomly sampled tool pages.

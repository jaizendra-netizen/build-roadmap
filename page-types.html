What every page type is actually called: and the day-by-day build order

Two things here. First, the naming: what each layer is called in real SEO vocabulary, what to put in your navigation, and what the URL should literally be. Second, the aggressive-but-safe 90-day calendar with every page assigned to a specific day. Plus the corrected service inventory. 1 · The vocabulary: and where your mental model needs one fix

Your specific URL questions, answered

"What should I call the services page?" Two things exist: the nav menu labeled "Services" (a dropdown, not a URL), and a services hub at /services/ that lists everything for humans and for internal linking. But the individual service pages live at the root, not nested: yourdomain.com/ai-agent-development-services/ · not /services/ai-agent-development-services/. Shorter URLs, and it's exactly what Appinventiv does (they keep a /service hub and put the real pages at root).

"Industries · is that a hub?" Yes. /industries/ is a hub page that lists all verticals; each vertical is a child at /industries/ai-agents-for-insurance/. Nested here is correct because the parent-child relationship is real and it keeps the URL self-describing.

"Case studies · hub?" Yes, same pattern: /case-studies/ hub, /case-studies/client-name/ children.

"Guide · do we really call it a pillar?" Yes, genuinely. "Pillar page" is standard vocabulary (from HubSpot's pillar-cluster model, now universal). Appinventiv literally uses /guide/… as the URL. Use /guide/ singular to match.

"Resources · link magnet?" The professional term is linkable asset; "link magnet" is the casual version. Both mean the same page type. URL: /resources/.

3 · "Will building this fast get me penalized?": the honest answer

No: and here's the precise distinction

Publishing 25–30 service pages at launch is not a velocity problem. Every agency website in the world launches with its complete service architecture; Google expects a business site to arrive whole. What got APPWRK and TechGropse demoted wasn't speed. It was thin, templated, near-duplicate pages with no unique value (APPWRK's Texas-suburb pages were 98–99% identical; TechGropse published 110 junk blog posts a month for years).

The line is quality, not calendar: if each of your 25 pages describes a genuinely different service, with your own architecture diagrams, your own client examples, and a real engineer's name on it, you can ship them all in three weeks safely. If you'd be swapping one word in a template 25 times, that's the danger zone regardless of how slowly you publish. Blog content is the one thing to pace steadily, because that's where the "mass-production" signal lives.

4 · The 90-day build calendar, day by day

Day 0: before a single page
  • Register domain · managed WordPress hosting (Kinsta/WP Engine/Cloudways) · SSL · lightweight theme (GeneratePress/Blocksy)
  • RankMath (or Yoast) · WP Rocket · Google Analytics 4 · Search Console + Bing Webmaster verified
  • The machine layer: robots.txt welcoming GPTBot/ClaudeBot/PerplexityBot/Googlebot by name · llms.txt · /ai-info/ · XML sitemap on with real lastmod · Organization schema sitewide
  • /book-a-call/ live with Calendly and nothing else on it
  • Decide the beachhead vertical (the one where 3+ of your 12 referral clients cluster)
Days 1–7, the spine
  • Homepage ("AI agent development company") · PARENT 1 /ai-agent-development-services/ · the flagship, 4,000+ words, Appinventiv template (services grid → Challenge/Solution/Impact proof strips → industries → process → FAQ)
  • /about/ with real team photos · /team/[each-SME]/ author pages for the 4–6 engineers who will byline content · /contact/
  • Kick off: client outreach for reviews (Clutch, GoodFirms, DesignRush profiles created now. They take weeks to populate)
  • Kick off: schedule the multi-client testimonial video shoot
Days 8–14: the money layer, wave 1
  • 6 child service pages under Parent 1: voice-agent · chatbot (KD 4) · copilot · custom · multi-agent · RAG
  • PARENT 2 /ai-automation-services/ + /business-process-automation-services/ (KD 8)
  • /services/ hub page listing everything
  • Each page: unique architecture diagram, one real client example, engineer byline, FAQ + FAQPage schema, single CTA to /book-a-call/
Days 15–21 · proof (the highest-converting week)
  • All 12 case studies: 4 deep (problem → architecture → measured result → client quote → video), 8 as structured cards
  • /case-studies/ hub · testimonial reel edited and embedded on homepage (self-hosted MP4, not YouTube)
  • Wire the bidirectional links: every case study ↔ its service page ↔ its industry page
Days 22–30, the wallet pages (your first rankings)
  • /guide/ai-agent-development-cost/ + the interactive calculator · the single highest-leverage page on the site, and a DR-3 competitor currently holds #1 for it
  • /ai-agent-consulting/ ($10 CPC) · /hire-ai-agent-developers/ ($13 CPC) · /managed-ai-agent-services/
  • /industries/ hub + beachhead vertical page + ecommerce + insurance
  • First 6 blog posts live (beachhead cluster), each linking up to its industry page
Days 31–45, depth and the first links
  • Remaining service pages: automation children (workflow, n8n, document-processing, integration) · consulting children (readiness assessment, governance, audit) · /dedicated-ai-team/, /ai-agent-poc-development/
  • /resources/ai-agents-in-production-2026/ · your original data from 50 engineers' deployments. This is the page that earns backlinks; give it real charts.
  • 2 more industry pages · 8–10 blog posts · /compare/best-ai-automation-agencies/ (KD 2, honest listicle)
  • Clutch/GoodFirms reviews should start landing, chase every client personally
Days 46–60, scale and geography
  • Programmatic layer v1: 40–60 /integrations/ai-agent-for-[tool]/ pages (Salesforce, HubSpot, Zendesk, Shopify, SAP, Slack, Jira…) · one template, real integration detail from your engineers, never empty stubs
  • First geo page /ae/ai-agent-development-company-dubai/ if UAE is a market
  • 10 blog posts · pillar pages /guide/ai-agents-for-business/ and /guide/enterprise-ai-agents/ (the year-two keyword, but the page needs to age)
Days 61–90 · compounding
  • Remaining verticals (healthcare, customer service) · /es/ /de/ /pl/ translations of the top 15 pages with hreflang
  • Programmatic layer v2 (another 40–60 integration/use-case pages)
  • 20–25 blog posts across clusters · second original-research drop
  • First refresh wave: everything built in days 1–30 gets reviewed, updated, dates bumped honestly
  • Start monthly prompt tracking: ask ChatGPT/Claude/Perplexity your 30 money questions, log who they cite. This is your AI rank tracker
Where you stand at day 90

~30 service pages, 12 case studies, 5–7 industry pages, 3 pillar guides, 2 linkable assets, 60–120 programmatic pages, ~45 blog posts, full GEO layer, live directory profiles with real reviews, and 3 months of founder content on a 200k-follower LinkedIn. That is materially bigger than intellectyx (rung 2) already, and you'd be starting to contest rung 3. The ceiling on this pace isn't Google. It's how many of your 50 engineers you can get to spend two hours a week writing down what they already know.

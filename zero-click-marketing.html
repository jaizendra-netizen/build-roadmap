Zero-Click Marketing: how founders win when Google, social & LLMs stop sending clicks

A structured, visual summary of the full talk — every number preserved — followed by the part you asked for: exactly what we take from it into our website + LinkedIn + Instagram plan. This talk is the strategic layer that explains everything our four site audits found.

1 · The diagnosis: the web now shows you, but doesn't send you

The "alligator graph": impressions keep rising, clicks keep falling — the jaws keep opening. It's structural, not a content problem (major publishers see the same). Search itself is growing — but a bigger share of searches end without any click.

The core reframe

"Your homepage has changed." Your first impression now happens on a Google snippet, inside a ChatGPT answer, in a LinkedIn post, in a Slack mention — before anyone visits your .com. Amanda's own example: she became a paying Formspree customer from inside a Claude response, without ever seeing their website. Your brand was either present on that surface or it wasn't.

2 · The second problem: your analytics are lying to you

  • Tracking is blind: ~30% cookie acceptance · 20–60% run blockers (60% for tech audiences) · 3.6 devices per person · GDPR/CCPA limits.
  • Dark social experiment (SparkToro, 1,100 tracked visits): traffic from TikTok, Slack, Discord, WhatsApp arrived labeled 100% "direct" — your Slack/WhatsApp wins are invisible in GA.
  • The Dropbox blackout study (peer-reviewed, IEEE): attributed ROAS said mobile ads = 1.53 and search ads = 2.0; causal ROAS (measured by switching channels off) was 0.7 and 0.92 — both actually underwater. Attribution overstates impact 2–10×. Dropbox moved $25M and improved LTV:CAC by 53%.
Why this matters to us

This is the answer to "how will we know the LinkedIn/Instagram work is driving the website leads?" We won't — not in analytics. We'll know by correlation (section 4), which is exactly how we'll report, and exactly the trap our future SEO hire must not fall into (optimizing only what's attributable).

Steps 1–4 created the demand; Google merely captured it. So the job is not just to rank — it's to influence the public record: reviews, Reddit threads, podcasts, third-party mentions, and the pages AI systems cite. Two proof stories from the talk:

  • Reddit outranks every B2B SaaS vendor simultaneously on 50–66% of shared keywords (Foundation Inc. analysis) — buyers form opinions in subreddits before visiting any vendor site. Be in those threads authentically or be invisible at the decision moment.
  • The Seer story: ONE negative review theme got repeated 67 times in AI outputs (ChatGPT, Perplexity amplified one data point into a "trend"). Fix: they published real retention data — after just 2 citations, the LLMs flipped. Caveat: it needed refreshing every few weeks. Lesson: if your proof lives in Slack or your head, it's not in the public record — and AI can only repeat what's published.

The banking mental model

Every zero-click post that delivers standalone value = a deposit (algorithmic goodwill + audience trust). The ask — subscribe, book a demo — is a withdrawal. Ratio: 5 deposits : 1 withdrawal. Don't make the click your only mechanism of value; make the value obvious in-feed, and the action obvious with a link when you do ask.

Q&A gold (tactical, verbatim-level)

  • LinkedIn cadence: 3×/week, not 2×/day — posting twice within 18–24h throttles your previous post; the algorithm rewards signal over volume.
  • Personal page beats company page for engagement — treat the company page as the news feed of record; the founder's page carries the point of view.
  • Text beats video on LinkedIn — her embedded-video reach runs 8–10× LOWER than text-only posts. (Video wins on Instagram.)
  • AI in content: use it as an editor/stress-tester ("poke holes, where is this weak?") — never the copywriter. The voice must be yours, or you forget what good is.
